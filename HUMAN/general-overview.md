## General Overview

The General Overview entries establish the conceptual and architectural foundations of the SpiritWiki as a Knowledge Ecosystem. The section opens with Vannevar Bush’s 1945 landmark As We May Think, which envisioned the “memex”—a desk-sized, microfilm-based personal library using associative indexing rather than rigid classification, thereby laying the conceptual groundwork for trail-based, non-hierarchical knowledge navigation. This visionary precursor is followed by Jack Park’s 2010 Boundary Infrastructure for IBIS Federation, which translates Bush’s associative trails into a concrete socio-technological blueprint for federated, collaborative sensemaking across distributed platforms, using semantic merging and agent-mediated coherence checking to transform isolated deliberations into a cumulative, navigable commons. The section culminates in Sosteric’s own framing essays—The SpiritWiki as Boundary Object and The SpiritWiki in Detail—which operationalize these historical and technical insights into a living system. Drawing on Star and Griesemer’s boundary-object theory, these pieces demonstrate how the SpiritWiki functions simultaneously as an ordered repository, an ideal type permitting diverse communities to project distinct meanings onto shared constructs, a coincident boundary aligning heterogeneous actors around common goals without demanding consensus, and a standardized form structuring communication while allowing local adaptation. Together, these entries argue that the SpiritWiki represents not merely an improved archive, but a dynamic, curated, and ontologically grounded knowledge ecosystem designed to democratize expertise, prevent canonical corruption, and serve as foundational infrastructure for planetary-scale human development.
